3. Why does constructive recovery work?

For user j, wⱼ = Qⱼ hⱼᴴ / √(hⱼ Qⱼ hⱼᴴ). This keeps that user’s desired received power. The residual Qⱼ − wⱼwⱼᴴ is positive semidefinite in exact arithmetic. Move all these residuals into the dedicated probing covariance and factor it: data plus probing still sum to the original R.

Because R and each desired power stay the same, total received power, SINR and the inverse-trace CRB stay the same (up to checked numerical defects). The principal-eigenpair baseline discards each data block’s remaining modes while retaining only the original probing covariance. It generally changes R and desired power, so feasibility can be lost.

Both designs share the same relaxation and power budget. Their actual used energies can differ. An infeasible or singular baseline is not a winning feasible design; no general superiority is claimed.

Scope and certificate limits

The full-response complex-MSE bound is Nr × radar noise / L × tr(R⁻¹). Singular R has no finite inverse-trace bound. The repaired dual certificate is floating-point evidence, not interval certification. Caps and cancellation do not prove infeasibility. Imported data is structurally checked but not independently recertified.

Source: Fan Liu et al., Cramér–Rao Bound Optimization for Joint Radar–Communication Beamforming, IEEE TSP 70 (2022), published Eqs. 46–49.
